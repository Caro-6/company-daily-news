# Canada Immigration Daily Briefing (2026-10-16)

## Today's Highlights

Today's top story: at noon today in Kitchener, Ontario, the Minister of Immigration announced a major Express Entry reform — **effective November 3, 2026**, candidates with eligible Canadian work experience or a qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation will receive additional CRS points. At the same time, **an LMIA will no longer be required** for these points or to meet job-offer requirements under the Federal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled Trades programs. Scores of candidates already in the pool will be recalculated automatically (those seeking job-offer points must update their profiles before November 3); all invitation rounds will be paused during recalculation (up to two weeks). Ontario also posted a provincial nominee update today (a new REDI round).

## Express Entry High-Wage Occupation Points (Effective November 3)

### What is changing

IRCC is adding a new CRS factor — the labour-market integration factor — worth up to 150 points (no spouse) or 140 points (with spouse). Two routes, whichever is higher, never combined:

**Route 1: Canadian work experience in a high-wage occupation** (on top of existing duration points)
- 12+ months in an occupation at 2x the national median wage: +70
- 12+ months at 1.5x or above: +40
- 12+ months at 1.3x or above: +20
- Combined with existing duration points (40 for 1 year, up to 80 for 5+ years), this route can reach 150 points

**Route 2: A qualifying job offer in a high-wage occupation**
- 2x median wage: 100 points; 1.5x: 70 points; 1.3x: 50 points
- Must generally be full-time, continuous work in Canada for at least one year after permanent residence; IRCC verifies each offer

**LMIA removed**: as of November 3, a qualifying job offer needs no LMIA for the new points, and a temporary public policy exempts candidates invited on or after November 3 from all LMIA-related requirements for job offers used under the Federal Skilled Worker and Federal Skilled Trades programs.

High-wage occupations are tiered at 1.3x / 1.5x / 2x the national occupational median wage; IRCC has published the official occupation list (about 186 occupations, to be updated regularly).

### Worked example: Canadian graduate + 1 year of Canadian work experience

Take a 29-year-old single applicant: Canadian bachelor's degree, CLB 9 language, 1 year of Canadian work experience in a software-type occupation. Current CRS: 469 (age 110 + education 120 + language 124 + Canadian experience 40 + skill transferability 75).

After November 3 (automatic recalculation, depending on the occupation's tier):
- 1.3x tier: 469 → **489**
- 1.5x tier: 469 → **509**
- 2x tier: 469 → **539**

Against the October 15 CEC round cutoff of 519: 509 is within striking distance, 539 clears it outright. Whether the change is transformative depends entirely on the occupation's tier — checking the list comes first.

### How to update your profile

- **Work-experience route: automatic recalculation, no action needed** — but it runs on your existing profile data, so verify NOC codes and employment dates are accurate before November 3.
- **Job-offer route: you must update your profile before November 3** with the required job-offer information, or you will not receive the points.
- After November 3, confirm your recalculated score; rounds will be paused for up to two weeks — no need to panic-edit your profile.
- IRCC will verify offer genuineness; a revoked offer no longer qualifies. Never misrepresent a NOC or job duties.

### Impact on the pool (projection)

IRCC's official pool distribution (September 13 snapshot, 226,793 candidates): 20,784 in the 501–600 band, 72,107 in 451–500, 61,560 in 401–450.

Under a central scenario (45% of candidates hold 12+ months of eligible Canadian work experience, half of them in high-wage occupations — a modelled estimate, not official data):
- ~51,000 candidates (22.5%) gain +20 to +70 points
- ~11,400 move from 500 or below into 501+
- The 501–600 band grows by roughly 47%; the 601+ band nearly triples

The direction is clear: upper bands will inflate significantly, and cutoffs for general and CEC rounds will likely rise once draws resume. Candidates below 400 with no bonus will effectively slide down the ranking.

### Who wins, who doesn't

- **In Canada + high-wage occupation + 1 year of experience**: the biggest winners, automatic points, nothing to do.
- **In Canada + non-high-wage occupation**: no bonus while cutoffs rise — the hardest-hit group.
- **Overseas, no Canadian experience**: no bonus; general rounds get tougher. French proficiency and PNP remain the pathways.
- **Holding a qualifying job offer**: 50–100 points with no LMIA needed — but update the profile before November 3.
- **PNP 600 points**: unaffected.

### Watch-outs

- Points follow the **occupation's** median wage, not your personal salary; tier boundaries create cliff effects.
- NOC is determined by job-offer duties — expect classification disputes; IRCC says it will verify.
- Removing the LMIA lowers the cost of fake offers; the strength of IRCC's anti-fraud checks is untested.
- The occupation list will be updated regularly — occupations can be removed, so plan with margin.
- The trend: Express Entry keeps getting more targeted, with "Canadian experience + high wage" as the central selection axis.
