# Canada Immigration Daily Briefing (2026-10-09)

## Today's Highlights

Official channels saw five updates today. At the federal level: the low-wage LMIA refusal-to-process CMA list was refreshed on its quarterly cycle, with 6 census metropolitan areas newly added to the frozen list (including Fredericton, New Brunswick) and 2 reopened, bringing the frozen total from 26 to 30; the IRCC newsroom issued one news release dated today (a business tax measure). Provincially: Newfoundland and Labrador issued a new ITA round on October 9 (131 invitations); the BC PNP added four targeted rounds on October 8; New Brunswick's Atlantic Immigration Program added selection rounds of September 25 and October 2 (63 applications selected for processing). No new Express Entry round was held.

## Announcements and Policy Changes

- Low-wage LMIA refusal-to-process CMA list refreshed (Rates updated: October 9, 2026)
  - New rates apply to applications submitted from October 9, 2026 to January 7, 2027; next quarterly update on January 8, 2027
  - Rule: low-wage LMIA applications with a work location in a CMA with an unemployment rate of 6% or higher will not be processed
  - Newly frozen (6 CMAs): Halifax (5.9% to 6.1%), Fredericton (5.3% to 6.2%), Kingston (5.3% to 6.3%), St. Catharines-Niagara (5.8% to 6.5%), Regina (5.9% to 6.7%), Lethbridge (5.4% to 6.0%)
  - Reopened (2 CMAs): Kamloops (7.0% to 3.6%), Chilliwack (7.9% to 5.6%)
  - Frozen total rises from 26 to 30
  - New Brunswick's three CMAs: Moncton stays frozen at 8.1%; Saint John stays open (5.9% to 5.5%); Fredericton is newly frozen (5.3% to 6.2%) — low-wage LMIA applications submitted in Fredericton from October 9 onward will not be processed
  - Source: Statistics Canada Labour Force Survey

  - Title: Minister Metlege Diab highlights new Productivity Mega Deduction to help businesses invest, grow, and create jobs in Canada
  - The release announces a business tax and investment measure (Productivity Mega Deduction), not an immigration policy change; it follows the same-topic media advisory of October 8
