# Canada Immigration Daily Briefing (November 12, 2026)

## Today's Highlights

On November 12, 2026, the federal government announced in Dieppe how the worker stream of the time-limited measure will work: provinces and territories will receive additional Provincial Nominee Program allocations to transition up to a combined total of 100,000 workers and their families to permanent residence in 2027. New Brunswick issued new versions of five immigration guides, adding general practitioners and social workers to the Skilled Worker priority occupations list. The federal government also updated program delivery instructions linked to the asylum reforms, covering designated representatives and the pre-removal risk assessment bar.

- The federal government set out how the worker stream of the time-limited measure will work: provinces and territories will receive additional Provincial Nominee Program allocations to transition up to a combined total of 100,000 workers and their families already in Canada to permanent residence in 2027, with the additional allocations to be used by March 31, 2027.
- New Brunswick issued November 2026 versions of three Skilled Worker guides; the priority occupations list grew from 33 to 35 occupations, adding general practitioners and social workers, and an employer reference letter is now a required document.
- New Brunswick issued an October 2026 version of the Express Entry Employment guide, and the Business Immigration Stream guide now lists new net-worth verification contacts.
- The federal government updated program delivery instructions linked to the asylum reforms, adding instructions on designated representatives so vulnerable applicants have a representative in pre-removal risk assessment and related removal proceedings.
- November 11 was a statutory holiday, with no new official publications; the latest federal Express Entry round remained Round 453 of October 28, 2026, and no new round was published that day.

## Guide Updates

- Program: New Brunswick Skilled Worker guides, three new versions
- Update date: November 2026
- Summary: the three guides (New Brunswick Experience, New Brunswick Graduates, and New Brunswick Priority Occupations) were issued as November 2026 versions. The guides were restructured, with process, fee and timeline details now set out on the official web pages, and the clause stating that the guide prevails where it differs from the website was removed. The priority occupations list grew from 33 to 35 occupations, adding general practitioners (NOC 31102) and social workers (NOC 41300), with no occupations removed. An employer reference letter is now a required document, to be submitted with a job offer or contract. The rule against combining language test scores was removed; the two-year validity of language test results remains. A Class 1 driver's licence is no longer a document that transport truck drivers must include with the application, and now falls under a general compliance requirement; the requirement for the employer to have been registered with New Brunswick's International Registration Plan for at least 24 months remains
- Analysis: the shorter guides do not mean looser requirements, as process and timeline details have moved to the official web pages, which must be read together with the guides. The two added occupations still require a job offer resulting directly from a Government of New Brunswick recruitment mission and one year of related paid work experience. Whether combined language test scores will be accepted is not yet stated, so the safe approach remains a single test result that meets the requirement

- Program: New Brunswick Express Entry Employment guide, new version
- Update date: October 2026
- Summary: the guide was issued as an October 2026 version, removing only the "Employer support" paragraph, with no other changes
- Analysis: the removed paragraph was explanatory text only, and the substantive requirements are unchanged

- Program: New Brunswick Business Immigration Stream guide, contact update
- Update date: file revised October 28, 2026, with the version label still March 2025
- Summary: the net-worth verification contact changed to Doane Grant Thornton LLP, contact Julie Carroll, telephone 902-566-6344; the MNP contact is unchanged, and nothing else in the guide changed
- Analysis: net-worth verification continues through Doane Grant Thornton or MNP, and applicants should use the new contact for verification

- Program: Federal program delivery instructions linked to the asylum reforms
- Update date: November 12, 2026
- Summary: following the asylum reforms that came into force on November 3, 2026, the federal government added instructions on designated representatives and updated the pre-removal risk assessment intake and oral hearing instructions. The designated representative provisions cover people under 18 and adults found unable to appreciate the nature of the proceedings, in pre-removal risk assessment, danger opinion and removal proceedings. A designated representative must be at least 18 years old, able to appreciate the nature of the proceeding and free of conflicts of interest; for minors the representative is usually a parent, and where no one is available the federal government contracts a representative. The pre-removal risk assessment bar guidance was updated to confirm that people whose refugee protection claims were determined to be withdrawn are also subject to the 12-month bar
- Analysis: this update puts the asylum reforms that came into force on November 3 into operational instructions, and its practical effect is on vulnerable people in pre-removal risk assessment and related removal proceedings; it has no direct effect on general economic or family class applicants

## Announcements and Policy Changes

- Program: Federal worker stream of the time-limited measure, how it will work
- Announcement date: November 12, 2026
- Announcement location: Dieppe, New Brunswick
- Summary: under the worker stream of the Budget 2026 time-limited measure, the federal government will offer provinces and territories additional Provincial Nominee Program allocations, which each jurisdiction may choose how much of to use, to transition up to a combined total of 100,000 workers and their families already in Canada to permanent residence in 2027. The additional allocations are intended to be used by March 31, 2027. Provinces and territories will also be able to identify workers in their jurisdictions who have already applied for permanent residence and are awaiting a decision, including through the Atlantic Immigration Program and federal programs and pilots closed to applications: the Start-up Visa Program, the Self-Employed Persons Program, the Agri-Food Pilot and home care worker programs. The workers concerned are already living in Canada and working in key industries, and the measure will not add significant new pressures on housing and community infrastructure. The measure is not a new pathway, takes no separate applications, runs for 2027 only, and provides additional space outside the 2027–2029 Immigration Levels Plan targets
- Analysis: with the worker stream mechanism now set out, allocations flow through additional Provincial Nominee Program space that provinces choose how much of to use, and March 31, 2027 is a planning date. Workers in Canada with a permanent residence application already in process, or who qualify under a Provincial Nominee Program, are the most direct beneficiaries; the practical step is to keep status valid and documents ready while provinces identify workers or use the allocations. The per-province allocations have not been published yet, and New Brunswick's share and how it will use it are still to be announced by the province
