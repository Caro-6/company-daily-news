# Canada Immigration Daily Briefing (November 9, 2026)

## Today's Highlights

On November 9, 2026, the federal government made the Rural Community Immigration Pilot a permanent program, Prince Edward Island issued 192 new invitations, Ontario updated its list of parties penalized under its immigration act, and a program delivery update restructured the Electronic Travel Authorization instructions.

- The federal government made the Rural Community Immigration Pilot a permanent program, with $29.1 million over three years and settlement support of up to $75,000 per person for up to 300 immigrants, and 5,500 admission spaces for 2027.
- Prince Edward Island issued 192 Labour and Express Entry invitations on November 9, bringing its 2026 total to 1,675.
- Ontario updated the published list of individuals, employers, and representatives who have had an administrative monetary penalty or banning order imposed for contravening the Ontario Immigration Act, 2015.
- A federal program delivery update restructured the Electronic Travel Authorization instructions. The change is format-only and existing guidance is unchanged.
- Federal Express Entry's latest round remained Round 453 of October 28, 2026, and no new round was published that day.

## Guide Updates

- Program: Federal Electronic Travel Authorization program delivery instructions restructure
- Update date: November 9, 2026
- Summary: the Visitors Program is restructuring the Electronic Travel Authorization program delivery instructions. The restructuring is format-only and does not change existing guidance content. It aligns the instruction format with other program areas and lays the groundwork for updating outdated content
- New instructions: Electronic Travel Authorization applications for Canadian citizen applicants and Canadian permanent resident applicants, and revalidating and inactivating an Electronic Travel Authorization
- Updated instructions: Temporary residents: Electronic travel authorization, Who is eligible for an Electronic Travel Authorization, and Manual Intervention
- Deleted instructions: Electronic travel authorization: Client service
- Analysis: this update announces no new rules, fees, effective dates, or affected groups, and current Electronic Travel Authorization rules for applicants are unchanged

## Announcements and Policy Changes

- Program: Federal Rural Community Immigration Pilot made a permanent program
- Announcement date: November 9, 2026
- Announcement location: Stellarton, Pictou County, Nova Scotia
- Summary: the Rural Community Immigration Pilot, launched in January 2025, becomes a permanent program, the Rural Community Immigration Program, giving skilled workers and international graduates in priority sectors a pathway to permanent residence. The government will invest $29.1 million over three years: $7.7 million in 2026–27, $13.1 million in 2027–28, and $8.3 million in 2028–29. Up to 300 immigrants will receive up to $75,000 each: $50,000 for settlement and integration and a $25,000 retention bonus, with the bonus requiring at least three years of residence in a rural community, or five years for Francophone candidates. The goal is to help up to 2,300 newcomers settle in rural communities by 2027, with 5,500 admission spaces for the program in 2027. The Francophone Community Immigration Pilot is extended to November 9, 2029. The related Ministerial Instructions take effect on November 10, 2026. As of August 31, 2026, the pilot had invited more than 650 candidates and more than 1,000 candidates had arrived. The 14 pilot communities are expected to continue in the permanent program, and expressions of interest from new communities will be sought in 2026. Communities can apply if they have a population under 200,000 and score 0.20 or higher on Statistics Canada's Index of Remoteness
- Analysis: the employer- and community-led rural immigration pathway becomes a lasting part of the system rather than a time-limited pilot. The three-year funding and settlement support of up to $75,000 per person show the focus is on retaining people, not only recruiting them, with the retention bonus tied to three years of residence, or five years for Francophone candidates. The 5,500 admission spaces for 2027 are already in the levels plan, and the Ministerial Instructions taking effect on November 10, 2026 put the new program rules in place. The Francophone Community Immigration Pilot is extended to November 9, 2029 at the same time

- Program: Ontario publishes updated list of orders for contraventions of the Ontario Immigration Act, 2015
- Update date: November 9, 2026
- Summary: the Ontario Immigrant Nominee Program updated the published names of individuals, employers, and representatives who have had an administrative monetary penalty or banning order imposed for contravening the Ontario Immigration Act, 2015. Information in an application must be accurate, correct, and not misleading. An administrative monetary penalty can reach $150,000 for each contravention and must be paid within 60 days of being served. Representatives who contravene the Act on or after December 19, 2024 can be banned for 3 to 10 years, or permanently if convicted of an offence under specified statutes while acting as a representative. Suspected fraud can be reported by email to programintegrityteam@ontario.ca
- Analysis: this is a program integrity publication update. It changes no eligibility criteria, scores, or allocations and does not affect applications already in process, but it signals that Ontario is publishing its enforcement decisions, and inaccurate information can lead to fines and bans
